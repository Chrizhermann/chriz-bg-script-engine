# What we learned about the engine (August–October 2026)

Reference for `chriz-bg-script-engine`, an EEex/Lua combat AI engine for BG2:EE that will build on Sword Coast Stratagems (SCS).
It is the short entry point to what is technically true; the memos in `docs/research/` hold the details. Names in parentheses are the memos to open for hashes, traces and exact ticks. The plan itself is `docs/plans/2026-10-14-combat-intent-on-scs.md`.
Short names: `scs-effective-mage-pilot` and `scs-high-mage-dispel` are the 2026-09-27 and 2026-09-28 memos. `scs-aoe-prebuff-delivery` and `aoe-prebuff-allies-design` are the 2026-08-30 memos on branch `codex/aoe-prebuff-design`. `PREP-03` is the failure, safety and compatibility threat model.

Tags: **[lab]** seen while the game ran in the disposable lab. **[src]** read from source, game files or generated scripts; nothing ran. **[host]** tested with plain Lua on the PC, not in the game. **[rule]** a rule we chose, not a measured fact.
Words: *registered* means we listed the actor on purpose. *Claim* means the new code takes one decision from the old script. *Shadow* means decide and record, never act. *Quarantined* means a saved record was set aside, not loaded. BCS is a compiled game script, BAF is its text form, SSL is the source language SCS compiles from.

Scope: every [lab] result comes from one stack: BG2:EE 2.7.3.0, EET 14.0, EEex 1.2.0 (LuaJIT), SCS 35.21, Spell Revisions (SR) 4.21-chriz.5 and Bubb's Spell Menu 5.2. The exceptions are marked "older stack" (BG2:EE 2.6.6.0: historical, re-check on 2.7.3.0 before relying on it) and "no-SR lab" (plain BG2:EE 2.7.3.0 with EEex 1.2.0 and SCS 35.21, install only).
Spell numbers and effects below are that stack's installed files. Read the effective files on any other stack. Nothing here is a support claim.
Credit: SCS is by DavidW, Spell Revisions by Demivrgvs and Gibberlings Three contributors, EEex by Bubb.

## 1. EEex and the engine at runtime

- **[src]** Exact EEex function names can be checked without calling them. A name that exists does not prove the signature, the native dependencies or a safe moment to call it. (EEEX-01)
- **[src]** `pcall` catches Lua errors only. A bad call on an engine object can crash the game, and Lua cannot catch that. (EEEX-01, PREP-03)
- **[src]** Calls we mapped from source: actor by session ID (`EEex_GameObject_Get`), party member by portrait (`EEex_Sprite_GetInPortrait`, party only), area scan (`EEex_Area_ForAllOfTypeInRange`), script slot read (`EEex_AIBase_GetScriptLevelResRef`), queue a response string (`EEex_Action_QueueResponseStringOnAIBase`) and save-data handlers (`EEex_Sprite_AddMarshalHandlers`). Several were later used in the lab. (EEEX-01)
- **[src]** Hooks: "initialized" fires at once if you register late. "Destroyed" fires after a game session is torn down. "Sprite loaded" fires after the effect list is read, so other fields may not be ready. "Action started" passes (sprite, action), skips NoAction and says nothing about completion. These lists only grow and we found no removal call, so re-running a bootstrap adds duplicates. (EEEX-01, EEEX-02)
- **[src]** We found no reviewed public tick, area-enter or save-start hook in EEex 1.2's Lua. That does not prove none exists. Do not use a busy stat or list hook as a scheduler without measuring how often it runs. (EEEX-01, EEEX-02)
- **[src]** EEex 1.2's installer labels its LuaJIT part experimental. Treat that as a reason to test, not as a known fault. (disposable-lab-provisioning)
- **[lab]** One root hook with swappable code behind it works. After 100 code reloads there was still one registration per hook. The game's own destroyed and loaded lists stayed at 3 and 4 entries, and those counts include other mods. (EEEX-03)
- **[lab]** Our autoload file (`M_…lua`) ran at startup, before the "initialized" dispatch. A save-then-reload and a return to the main menu each fired the destroyed event. An area change was only seen by sampling afterwards. Order against other mods was not tested. (EEEX-03)
- **[lab]** `EEex_Utility_GetMicroseconds()` is a usable microsecond clock. `os.clock` is too coarse. The game ran about 15 world ticks per second (150 ticks in about 10 seconds), and our callback came about every 33 ms. (PERF-01, KERNEL-01)
- **[lab]** Looking costs time and grows with actor count. One observer snapshot (area scan plus plain-number reads) took a median of about 70 µs with 1 actor, 140 µs with 5 and 600–650 µs with 33. The worst sample was 8 ms. Adding our eligibility checks, copies and trace roughly doubled the cost of a scan plus two actor reads, from about 380 µs to 680–780 µs. (PERF-01, KERNEL-01)
- **[lab]** A full two-mage shadow pass (sense, candidates, score, choose, trace) took a median of 8.5 ms and a p95 of 18 ms in a small scene. In the active tests, recorder callbacks had a median near 1 ms. The slowest single callbacks took 51–104 ms, with verbose logging on. (mage-prebuff-shadow-results, active-combat-soak)
- **[rule]** Never save userdata, session object IDs, stored scripting-target IDs, plans, targets, reservations, claims, in-flight attempt records or queue ownership. Save plain numbers and declared string names only. After any load, look actors up again. (EEEX-01, EEEX-02, EEEX-06, PREP-03)
- **[src]** EEex's serializer picks an integer width for each number and ends strings at a NUL byte. Fractions get no integer check there, and an embedded NUL changes a string's length when it is read back. Our profile avoids both. (EEEX-06)
- **[host]** Our save profile accepts whole numbers from 0 to 4294967295, NUL-free strings, records and bounded arrays. It rejects cycles, metatables, functions and userdata. It also rejects booleans, so flags are 0 and 1. EEex 1.2.0 can store booleans; we chose 0 and 1 for portability. (EEEX-06)
- **[lab]** Saved values round-tripped, including 255, 256, 65535, 65536 and 4294967295. Old versions migrated step by step. A future-version record and a record with a bad value were each quarantined alone while their neighbours loaded. "Bad value" means a wrong number inside valid data, not damaged save bytes. (EEEX-06)
- **[lab]** A named load fires the destroyed event. That cleared our short-lived state before any saved data was imported. (EEEX-06)
- **[lab]** If our handler, or all our modules, are missing, EEex keeps our data in a fallback store on the actor. Nothing was migrated or classified meanwhile. A save made without the handler brought all four records back once the handler returned. That test loaded a clean baseline first. Adding a handler onto an already-loaded fallback was not shown to be safe. (EEEX-06)
- **[lab]** `Infinity_GetINIValue` returns numbers. A value set with `Infinity_SetINIValue` reads back at once and was in the profile's `Baldur.lua`, as a string, after the game was stopped by force, so it is not written only at a clean exit. (HOOK-01)
- **[rule]** Do not read sprite fields inside the "sprite loaded" callback. A first draft read a name mid-load. Safety was unproved, so the probe now ignores the argument and samples later. (EEEX-03)
- **[lab]** An area keeps its creatures in render lists. `m_lVertSort` (front) holds the standing ones, held, stunned and confused included. Sleeping creatures lie in `m_lVertSortBack` with the dead. `m_lVertSortFlight` and `m_lVertSortUnder` exist too. EEex's area search (`GetAllInRange`, behind `EEex_Sprite_ForAllOfTypeStringInRange`) walks the front list only: in the step-3 lab it found none of 56 sleeping-dummy samples and no corpse. (DIRECTOR-01)
- **[lab]** Effect fields read in game: `m_effectId`, `m_effectAmount`, `m_dWFlags`, `m_durationType` and `m_res:get()`. (DIRECTOR-01)
- **[lab]** Effect timing in a creature's lists (`m_timedEffectList`, `m_equipedEffectList`): with `m_durationType` 4096, `m_duration` holds the game tick the effect ends at, so the seconds left are (`m_duration` − `m_gameTime`)/15. Types 1 and 9 are permanent (`m_duration` 0) and 2 is equipped. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[lab]** The engine counts what is left of two protections: `m_nMirrorImages` on the sprite holds the Mirror Images (5 read on an SCS mage), and stat 88 (STONESKINS) the skins (6 after SCS's Stoneskin of 6). (DEFENCE-01)
- **[lab]** SCS's pre-buffs come from DW copies of the spells: the effects on its mage named `DWSW212` (Mirror Image), `DWSW408` (Stoneskin) and the like as their source, never the memorized `SPWI` file. Whether a buff is up must be read from its effects, not matched by the spell's name. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[lab]** Combat stats read in game (this stack's STATS.IDS): 2 armour class, 7 THAC0 (13 for the level-8 fighters), 8 attacks a round (2 with a sword and 3 with a bow; IESDP: 6 to 10 mean half attacks, from 1/2 to 4 1/2), 84 THAC0BONUSRIGHT and 86 DAMAGEBONUSRIGHT. The right-hand bonuses changed with the weapon (3 and 2 with a sword, 6 and 4 with a bow) and appear to hold strength, proficiency and the weapon's own bonus. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[lab]** The weapon in hand: `m_equipment.m_selectedWeapon` is its slot (35, the first weapon slot; 11, the first quiver slot, while shooting a bow, so the item read is the arrow) and `m_selectedWeaponAbility` its ability. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[lab]** Opcode 72 with parameter 1 set to 128 sets both `m_typeAI.m_EnemyAlly` and `m_liveTypeAI.m_EnemyAlly` to 128 (NEUTRAL). SCS still cast at the neutral PC once in ten fights. (DIRECTOR-01)

## 2. Actions and casting

- **[src]** The queue helper parses a response string and appends it. It tells you nothing about start or completion. (EEEX-01, ACT-01)
- **[host]** Each request is tracked as proposed, accepted, started, advanced and completed. It can also end failed, interrupted, invalidated, timed out or unknown. "Completed" needs proof from the world. A queue return, an action start, a new action or an empty queue is not proof. (ACT-01)
- **[rule]** A time-out, a lost actor or a load ends our claim. It does not mean cancelled, refunded or safe to retry. We keep watching, never clear another producer's queue and never resubmit. (ACT-01)
- **[lab]** Ordinary self-cast: the queue held exactly one action-31 entry with the right spell, the start callback fired next tick, and later one memorized copy was spent (2 to 1) and the new timed effect was present. We needed all of these. (ACT-02)
- **[lab]** The slot is spent within a few ticks of the start, about 20 ticks before the effect shows (seen with Mirror Image and with Strength). Spending is not completion. (ACT-02)
- **[lab]** A start callback does not mean a cast. With a busy aura the callback fired at once but the slot was spent 80 ticks later. Under silence the entry and callback happened, yet no slot was spent and no effect came in 450 ticks. (ACT-02)
- **[lab]** Silence shows in the active derived state (flag 4096), not in base state. The quick-list "disabled" flag stayed false under silence. (ACT-02)
- **[src]** `EEex_Sprite_GetCastTimer` returns -1 when the aura is free, so 0 does not mean free. A nil sprite returns 0, so the number alone does not prove the actor is valid. (EEEX-01, ACT-02)
- **[host]** A caster's usable spells are its ready memorized copies that also appear in its usable quick buttons. (COMBAT-02)
- **[lab]** One point of damage during windup ended the action next tick. The slot stayed spent, no effect came, no refund was seen and no "interrupted" reason was given. We infer the interruption. One case only. (ACT-03)
- **[lab]** A started cast that a move replaced spent no copy in one run and one copy in two others. A start does not prove a debit. We accept zero or one copy and record which. (active-combat-soak, ACT-04)
- **[lab]** When the target died mid-cast, the caster's action ended early and the slot stayed spent. No effect was seen in the six samples where the target could still be found, the last one dead. After that the target could not be found at all, even by a lookup that includes corpses. So disappearing is not proof of death; HP 0 with state flag 2048 was. Delivery after that point is unknown. (ACT-03)
- **[lab]** When the target walked away mid-cast, the spell still landed, after the target had stopped. Delivery to a moving target was not tested. (ACT-04)
- **[lab]** An action appended behind a started cast did not cancel it. A running legacy script block did not pre-empt it. An `ActionOverride(…, MoveToPoint(…))` sent to the caster did replace it: the slot stayed spent and no effect came in 301 samples. Who wins depends on how the new action arrives. When we lose, we drop our claim and do not restore the old queue. (ACT-04)
- **[lab]** Loading a game ends the attempt. Two saves taken mid-action (Wait then Move; a started cast) resumed nothing in 451 to 961 ticks after load. The saved cast's spent slot stayed spent, with no refund. We did not decode the queue bytes in the save. (ACT-03)
- **[lab]** Moving the player to another area ended our view of an NPC caster. On return it was idle, the slot was spent and the target had no effect. What happened while away is unknown. (ACT-03)
- **[lab]** A Project Image owner reports owner type 2 (stat 139) while its copy lives; we made the copy with a forced setup cast. We queue nothing on it. After the copy died, the owner type went back to 0 and an ordinary cast worked. Check stat numbers in the installed STATS.IDS: here 139 is PUPPETMASTERTYPE, 140 PUPPETTYPE and 141 PUPPETID. (ACT-02)
- **[lab]** Shield leaves an opcode-328 effect (state 126). Mirror Image leaves opcodes 159 and 206 and no 328. A generic Haste state test stayed false on visible Haste (older stack). Test each buff by its own effect source. (mage-prebuff-shadow-results, scs-effective-mage-pilot, scs-aoe-prebuff-delivery)
- **[lab]** In these tests SPWI212 is Mirror Image, SPWI214 is Strength and SPWI112 is Magic Missile. That is this stack's installed files, not a vanilla guess. Do not generalize to other actors, spells, stacks or contested queues. (ACT-02, COMBAT-01)
- **[rule]** Decisions use ordinary queued casts (`SpellRES`). Never use forced casts or injected effects for them. `ReallyForceSpellRES` is documented as instant and uninterruptible, and it does not spend the memorized spell. (active-combat-soak, scs-aoe-prebuff-delivery)
- **[src]** EEex's started-action listener runs right after the engine sets the action (`CGameSprite::SetCurrAction`), never for NoAction, and runs its listeners in registration order. (DIRECTOR-01)
- **[lab]** A starting cast can take another target. In that listener, setting `action.m_acteeID.m_Instance` made a Spell (31) cast land on the new target, with its normal casting time and one memorized copy spent (Magic Missile, Acid Arrow). (DIRECTOR-01)
- **[lab]** A cast at a kind of creature (`SpellRES("…",[PC.0.0.MAGE])`) starts with `m_Instance` -1, because the engine decodes the object only as the cast runs. `EEex_Object_EvalAsAIBase(action.m_acteeID, sprite, false)` decodes a copy and names the creature. `action.m_acteeID:Set(target:virtual_GetAIType())` changes the target: `m_Instance` then reads the new id, and the cast landed there. (DIRECTOR-01)
- **[lab]** SCS's `castspell` round timer holds the game tick it runs out at; `EEex_Sprite_GetLocalInt` reads it. A response the engine queued with `SetGlobalTimer("castspell","LOCALS",ONE_ROUND)` set it 90 ticks ahead, so `ONE_ROUND` resolves in a queued response. Two actions in one queued response ran in order, in the same tick, when a line break separated them. (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** `SpellRES("…",EEex_Target("BGSE"))` cast at the creature stored in the caster's own `EEex_Target` table, and `SpellPointRES("…",[x.y])` started at exactly that point; both spent the spell. (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** The casting aura: `EEex_Sprite_GetCastTimer` counts from 0 at a cast's start to about 99, about one a tick, then reads -1 again, so roughly 100 ticks. SCS's `castspell` timer counts 90 ticks from the cast's queueing, so the aura nearly always outlasts it. A Spell action that starts while the aura runs becomes the current action at once and casts when the aura ends; this was seen at aura counts of 41 to 97. It restarts at 0 with each new cast. It stands still while the creature is frozen by another creature's Time Stop (below), apart from one step for each action the frozen creature's script starts. (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** The hook's `EEex_LuaAction` (action 472) normally starts and acts within a tick. It waits only while the creature is frozen by Time Stop (next entry). (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** Time Stop (Spell Revisions' SPWI909, opcode 231) set the game's `m_nTimeStop` to 539 and `m_nTimeStopCaster` to the caster's id (both on `EngineGlobals.g_pBaldurChitin.m_pObjectGame`). The count fell by two a tick, so time stood still for about 270 ticks (18 seconds). Only the caster acted. Every other creature froze: no action executed, the engine's hook action included. Creatures caught idle, walking, waiting or holding the hook action kept running their scripts every 16 ticks, so SCS's blocks queued casts again each round and the engine's gate claimed; creatures caught in the middle of a cast ran no script until time resumed. The hook action acted when time resumed, on the creature's latest claim. Frozen creatures read `m_bForceEffectProcessFromTimeStop` 1 and `m_derivedStats.m_bImmuneToTimeStop` 0. (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** In batch E every cast the engine queued started (became the current action) within a tick, so the engine never had to hold a queued cast. Scripts did reach their hook while a Spell action was current: a mage's script asked every 16 ticks during one. Batches E2 to E4 (171 more casts, in E3 and E4 often queued while the casting aura still ran) needed no hold either. (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** The engine's defences (D1) ran in 36 fights against party-side fighters with their own scripts (batches F2 and F3): reading what protects each friend, the threat picture and the decision ran without an error or a failed read. Its one defence, Defensive Harmony, reached two allies, and both carried it afterwards. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[lab]** In those fights the group's disables kept the fighters away. After a lab Breach stripped the casters' weapon protections, the fighters were disabled 73 to 89% of the time when the engine cast, and 10% (high group) or 72% (mid) with SCS alone. Against these 250-hit-point fighters the engine cast mostly disables, and the high group's fights lasted about 110 seconds, against 65 with SCS alone. (DEFENCE-01)

## 3. The BCS to Lua bridge

- **[src]** EEex's trigger and action helpers (`EEex_Trigger.lua`, `EEex_Action.lua`) set the executing object, then run the Lua text they are given. A script block asks Lua whether to claim. If so, the response runs a Lua action. We pass only the actor's plain ID, for same-session comparison. (BCS-01)
- **[lab]** Declining falls through. If the module is absent or disabled, a capability is missing, the policy returns false or raises an error, or the public Lua root is gone, there is no claim. The next legacy block runs as if we were not there. We saw each of these. (BCS-01)
- **[lab]** Order still decides. If a higher block takes the action and stops, our block is never reached. If the higher block continues, ours is reached and can claim. (BCS-01)
- **[lab]** The claiming block must not end in `Continue()`. With it, the legacy fallback ran as well in the first session. Removing only that `Continue()` fixed it in two later nine-case runs. (BCS-01)
- **[lab]** An explicit re-arm produced a fresh claim and a separate Lua action note. (BCS-01)
- **[host]** In Lua text, `_G[[B-]]` is a call with a string argument, not an index. Write `_G[ [[B-]] ]`. The installer test checks the payload bytes inside the compiled script. (BCS-01)
- **[rule]** Keep the order of the actor's existing blocks. Do not put a bridge above `NoAction()` staging, change slots at runtime or hard-code SCS script names. Higher encounter control always wins. (PREP-03)
- **[src]** Where the hook goes in SCS scripts: directly before the first block that tests SCS's `castspell` round timer, skipping first-round preparation and the ranged fighters' retarget block. Bookkeeping runs and abilities cast without a spellbook check that sit below it are copied above the hook behind the same claim; the originals stay, so a declining engine leaves SCS's order untouched. (HOOK-01)
- **[lab]** In the lab game 1,028 SCS scripts took the hook (585 mage, 307 priest, 136 general AI) with 799 copied blocks. WeiDU's result matched the Python rule block for block, and every other script kept its bytes. Decompile plus recompile of all SCS scripts changes no byte by itself. (HOOK-01)
- **[lab]** No-SR lab, install only: 718 SCS scripts took the hook (416 mage, 192 priest, 110 general AI) with 764 copied blocks; the same two scripts were left alone. WeiDU matched the Python rule block for block on all 718, and the uninstall left every file byte-identical. Fixed-name scripts hooked in both labs got the same anchor and copies; bookkeeping copies use the same SCS helper items. The rule does not depend on SR. (HOOK-01)
- **[lab]** No-SR lab re-check with the monster hook and answer 10 (install only): 823 scripts took the hook (416 mage, 192 priest, 110 general AI, 105 monster) with 2,672 copies, against 2,178 with the monster hook alone. The new copies are 200 `SpellNoDec` casts and 294 target picks in 31 scripts. They include all 105 casts of the original Melissan (`meliss01`–`03`), whose summons and undead kill stay copied as blocks beyond a combat round. WeiDU matched the Python rule on all 823, the registry listed exactly them, and the uninstall left every file byte-identical once `WeiDU.log` was put back. (HOOK-01)
- **[src]** The step-1 rule's specials are `ForceSpell`-family casts without a spellbook check. Scripted bosses also act through `SpellNoDec` without a spellbook check, summons and `ActionOverride`. Without Ascension, SCS scripts the original Melissan (`meliss01`–`03`) with its general-AI settings, so she is hooked, and under the step-1 rule her spell list, summons and undead kill stayed below the hook; with Ascension only one Illasera self-heal is affected. The monster-hook rule also copies blocks whose actions go beyond a combat round, such as summons. Since Christopher's answer 10, `SpellNoDec` and `SpellNoDecRES` casts without a spellbook check are specials too, unless the block removes the spell: in the SR lab that adds 130 copies in 37 scripts (fiends, two dragons, Ascension's bosses, one general AI) and 207 target picks, 3,003 copies in all; WeiDU still matches the Python rule on all 1,148 scripts. (HOOK-01, HOOK-02)
- **[lab]** Six installed SCS creatures (two mages, a priest, two fighters, a clay golem): with the hook declining, SCS's fight ran as usual; with the engine holding every round, round casts and attacks stopped while prebuffs, the first-round sequencer and the golem's copied haste still ran; the family and global switches handed creatures back to SCS at once. (HOOK-01)
- **[lab]** The claim is answered once per creature and tick. In one 60-second fight the copies and the hook asked 149 more times in the same pass and got the cached answer. (HOOK-01)
- **[lab]** In fights, a mid-level SCS caster's script reached the hook about every 16 ticks, with gaps of 48 to 128 ticks while the creature cast. Anything that learns from hook passes sees nothing new from a creature during its casts. (DIRECTOR-01)
- **[lab]** A declining hook costs no tick: it is a false condition, so SCS's next block runs in the same script pass. With the hook declining, SCS's casters started their next cast 5–9 ticks after the round timer ran out whenever they had something to cast. No run without the hook has been timed yet. (HOOK-01)
- **[host]** One declined check costs about 2.4 µs on host LuaJIT, including compiling the trigger text as EEex does on every call; a tick is about 67 ms. When the engine claims a round and then fails to act, that script pass is lost, and after an error the creature goes back to SCS for 150 ticks. (HOOK-01, `kernel/hook.lua`)
- **[lab]** With the engine holding, SCS's Energy Blades bookkeeping ran from its guarded copy above the hook: twice, each time swapping the blade item for SCS's helper item and setting `eneblade` to 10. It waited until the mage's Time Stop cast ended; a claimed hook does not cut into a started cast. (HOOK-01, ACT-04)
- **[host]** The bridge installs as a normal WeiDU component and publishes exactly four authored files. It refuses to overwrite a file of the same name. In a fake game, install, reinstall and uninstall left other resources byte-identical. Uninstall does not shrink the language TLK. (BCS-01, compatibility-lab-results)
- **[lab]** Ordinary dialogue worked with the bridge disabled (six paths). A missing object selector fell back to Player1. `DialogInterrupt(FALSE)` on the recipient did not stop a conversation opening. (BCS-01)
- **[lab]** A queued dialogue start was cancelled by an ordinary move override. It was lost when the player left the area and came back, and nothing resumed it. An already-open dialogue was never interrupted in tests. (BCS-01)

## 4. SCS: what is fixed at install and what runs

- **[src]** At install, SCS expands its SSL source to BAF, then compiles it to BCS. Its compiler is a Perl script or a Windows exe. Component 6030 (mages) needs 5900. 6040 is priests. (SCS-01, SCS-shared-rule-expansion, scs-aoe-prebuff-delivery, priest-turn-shadow-results)
- **[src]** Per creature, SCS picks type, level and spells and builds a script. If the creature has no script name yet, SCS picks one, strips old SCS scripts and inserts the new one in a low slot. A supplied name skips that step. (SCS-01, SCS-shared-rule-expansion)
- **[src]** At run time only the compiled scripts exist. The generator's inputs are gone: spell tables, kit, level, settings, random picks and library order. The script and the creature file cannot give them back. (SCS-01, SCS-05, scs-high-mage-dispel)
- **[src]** Names: the generator searches for an unused `dw#mg…` name, so the name is not a stable identity. In the lab copy a level-6 mage uses `dw#mg266`, a level-18 mage `dw#mg256` and Kruin `dw#mg224`. Never find SCS actors by name. (SCS-01, scs-effective-mage-pilot, scs-high-mage-dispel, SCS-05)
- **[src]** Slots: the generated script goes in the default slot, the lowest one, after override, class, race and general. Low insertion does not just fill an empty slot. It can shift other scripts toward a hole, or merge the bottom two. One mage had override empty and default `dw#mg266`. Kruin had override `gpshout`, general `kruin`, default `dw#mg224`. (SCS-shared-rule-expansion, scs-effective-mage-pilot, SCS-05)
- **[src]** Order: block order is priority, and `Continue()` lets evaluation go on. Weights such as 100:50 are odds between responses inside one block. The second response is the one that skips the cast: it holds only the on-continue actions and `Continue()`. They are not measured frequencies. A level-6 mage script has 239 blocks and 128 `Continue()` actions. A level-18 mage has 479 and 320. (scs-effective-mage-pilot, scs-high-mage-dispel, SCS-shared-rule-expansion)
- **[src]** Mage template order: setup, non-hostile guards, contingency, escape, melee, defensive renewal, easy mode, pursuit, then 47 core-magic includes, then closedown. Early dispel comes before many offensive choices. Breach is the ninth include. (SCS-02, scs-high-mage-dispel)
- **[src]** The rule set differs by creature. The level-6 mage script has no early-dispel block and no named-encounter branch. The level-18 mage script has early dispel. The memos read this as generation-time pruning, not as obsolete rules. (scs-effective-mage-pilot, scs-high-mage-dispel)
- **[src]** Source order is not output order. In the Combine path, nearest-first target lists come out reversed: early dispel lists the third-nearest PC first, and Kruin's Symbol Stun lists six selectors in reverse. Breach's repeated full blocks keep nearest first. (SCS-shared-rule-expansion, scs-high-mage-dispel, SCS-05)
- **[src]** More output surprises. An action written before the main action in source can come out after it (Kruin's helper cast and removal follow his cast). Staged `See(...)`/`False()` selector blocks precede action blocks. Alternatives can be pruned at generation. (SCS-shared-rule-expansion, scs-high-mage-dispel, SCS-05)
- **[src]** Libraries append. Ordinary `TARGET`/`TRIGGER` entries add to a body and only `*_REPLACE` entries clear it. SR's comment-only `SIAbjuration` entry leaves the base immunity test in place, while SR's blank replacement removes `SIDivination`. ## 8. Known gaps and open questions

- True quickload is unproved. No callable QuickLoad was found, so tests use a named load. The stats "QuickLoad" only reloads sprite stats. (EEEX-06)
- We saw nothing resume after a load, but never decoded the queue bytes inside a save. Whether a save holds an in-flight action is unknown. We also never moved a casting actor between areas; only the player moved. (ACT-03)
- Interruption has no native reason code. We saw one damage case and one imposed silence. Other causes and other casters are untested. There is no general way yet to tell who damaged whom. (ACT-03, ACT-02, active-combat-soak)
- Real player-click replacement, true action ownership, and two producers sending the same shape of request are unresolved. (ACT-04)
- Hooks: no per-actor destruction callback, no scheduler tick, no cadence data, no field-safety data for half-loaded objects, no other EEex builds, no optional-module removal test. (EEEX-03)
- No native crash was seen in this project's runs. One would bypass all Lua error handling. The rule is to stop at the first crash and keep the exact call. (PREP-03, EEEX-03)
- Not tested: missing, mixed or removed EEex; compiled bridge files left behind when EEex goes; a cold full-package install; uninstalling mid-state. Partial EEex removal with bridges left behind is treated as unsupported, not as safe fall-through. (compatibility-lab-results, PREP-03)
- The kernel's "environment complete" flags were set by hand in tests. No real dependency or public-state discovery exists yet. (KERNEL-01, compatibility-lab-results)
- One main stack, plus the older-stack Haste probe, and every game run used Spell Revisions. Spell numbers, effect layouts and counts come from it. Only the hook install was checked without SR; no game ran without SR or on another SCS version. (ACT-02, EEEX-03, scs-aoe-prebuff-delivery, HOOK-01)
- Close SCS reading covers three installed mage scripts (levels 6 and 18, and Kruin) and one priest script. The 47 mage includes are identity-checked but mostly not mapped to behavior. No census of encounter exceptions exists. (SCS-02, SCS-05, scs-high-mage-dispel, priest-turn-shadow-results)
- Run-time unknowns in SCS: who writes `offscreenprep` (three reads, no writer in the script read), area and controller scripts, shouts, `See`/`False` side effects, empty party slots and real response frequencies. (scs-effective-mage-pilot, scs-high-mage-dispel)
- Whether SCS's Windows `ssl.exe` matches the Perl source we read is unknown. (SCS-shared-rule-expansion)
- Prebuff accounting in play: SCS's forced helper plus removal has not run on an assigned SCS actor. Interrupts, death and loads between the two steps, and helper-on-helper stacking, are unobserved. Haste was cast twice by hand on an older stack, with one ally and no radius edge, hostile, neutral or summon. (scs-aoe-prebuff-delivery)
- Area spells have no proven recipient model, and Slow and Glitterdust landing is unknown. (SPACE-02, mage-prebuff-shadow-results)
- Movement has no route or clearance measure. The short stop at (264,258) is explained only as "it also happens without our AI". (SPACE-01, active-combat-soak)
- Priest Turn: how the `See` trigger picks its target, timer equality edges, disabled-target behaviour and callback cost are unresolved. (priest-turn-shadow-results)
- Cost was measured on small scenes. There is no encounter-scale or long-run test, no production budget, and memory growth was not attributed. (PERF-01, mage-prebuff-shadow-results)
- Production handoff: the hook goes into the same SCS script, before the per-round choice (HOOK-01). How to prove no outside controller owns the actor is still open. (PREP-03)
- The hook rule was checked without Spell Revisions by install only, not in a fight. In lab fights the engine has only declined or held; specials ran for a golem, a tanar'ri and a vampire, and the arbitration for one creature with two hooked scripts and one with an unhooked script below. (HOOK-01, HOOK-02)
- Cost per pass in game: a monster script asks the engine up to about 17 times a pass, and the first ask reads the creature's script slots. Not timed in game. (HOOK-02)
- Toolchain: the decision memo named WeiDU v251.00, which was unavailable then. Later fixture and decompile work used build 24900. Which one the project pins is undecided. (host-runtime-toolchain-decision, ACT-02)
- Save isolation is shown for the save and load path only. Earlier runs, OneDrive and remote saves were not audited. Some early runs saw save folders with real-playthrough names appear in the lab profile; the route is unproven. (disposable-lab-runbook)
- Licensing: the SCS readme allows code reuse with full credit and asks that SCS not be mirrored. No SCS-derived data is approved for outside release, and no SCS source or generated script is committed. (SCS-pilot-oracle)
- Difficulty has no setting sweep and no fairness measure. (SCS-06)
- Timing: SCS's delay from its round timer to the next cast was measured only with the hook installed. A run without the hook is still missing. (HOOK-01)
- The director perceives a lying creature with a search run from where it lies. In the lab, every sleeping-dummy sample (164) entered the group's picture that way. Whether that matches the engine's own sight (`See()`, object selection) is untested. Creatures in the flight and under lists are not perceived at all. (DIRECTOR-01)
- Decoding a copy of a cast's object at its start is not proven to name the creature the engine picks later, when positions may have changed. A sleeping creature named that way may need `checkBackList` set. (DIRECTOR-01)
- The engine's own casts met enemies that fight back only in batches F2 and F3, four party-side fighters (and in F2 a mage) with their own scripts. Whether a cast landed is judged for single-target casts only; area casts teach the memory nothing yet. (CHOICE-01)
- The engine's defences have not met real pressure: no caster came near death in batches F2 and F3, so their worth, the caution knob, the acute-danger exception and the interruption risk are untested in a fight. The spell threat's numbers are guesses. (DEFENCE-01)
- Whether a hook pass that holds the round can clear a queued cast is untested: in batches E to E4 no queued cast had to wait for its start. (CHOICE-01)

## 9. Spell and item data

- **[host]** The classifier (`combat-intent/spells.lua`) reads what an ability does from its real effects and follows spells and EFF files cast by effects. Over every SPL it found roles for 4,614 of the SR lab's 6,746 spells and 2,090 of the no-SR lab's 2,426; the rest hold only graphics, sounds or bookkeeping. Unknown opcodes: EEex's 401 and 402 and HP swap (343) in the SR lab, none without SR. 70 SR-lab spells and one no-SR spell are partial, nearly all because they name SPL files that are not installed. Host census only; nothing ran in game. (SPELLS-01)
- **[src]** Opcode traps for readers: parameter 2 of opcode 0 and 332 is a damage type, not a modifier type; 178 and 179 keep a signed amount in the special field; positive casting-time values (189) cast faster; 135 on oneself is a shapechange; the creature holding a time stop (231) is immune to it; 177 and 283 with IDS entry 0 apply to anyone; 146 type 1 ignores its casting level; 333 without a resource casts the holder's resref plus B. Only save-type bits 0–4 are saves. (SPELLS-01)
- **[lab]** Spell Revisions' sub-spell effects name the sub-spell as their source: Glitterdust's effects on the dummies carried `SPWI224E` (opcodes 54, 101, 142, 146, 174, 206, 328), never `SPWI224`. Whether a spell landed must accept every spell and EFF file it delivers through. (CHOICE-01)
- **[src]** SR's Cone of Cold and Shout pause their own caster for two seconds (opcode 165 on the caster). That is a cost of casting, not harm. (CHOICE-01)
- **[src]** A 177 or 283 with an IDS entry delivers only to creatures of that type; the rest of the spell reaches everyone. SCS adds troll extras to spells this way: a 177 for race 129 delivering DWTROLFI, DWTROLAC or DWTROLCO, in 191 of the SR lab's spell files. Spell Revisions uses it for exclusions. Dire Charm's 177s give elves (at 90%) and half-elves (at 30%) an immunity to the charm opcode before the charm comes. The charm sub-spell DVHCHRM first protects elves and half-elves from itself, and Horror's sub-spell first protects the undead from Horror. A reader that turns such an entry into a filter on the whole spell locks these spells away. (CHOICE-01)
- **[src]** A delivery can carry the saving throw. SR's Charm Person charms through two 177s, one for each side of EA 200 (199 NOTEVIL, 200 EVILCUTOFF), and the saves sit on them (−5 and −9 in its last ability), not on the charm. (CHOICE-01)
- **[src]** Most SR spells begin with an opcode 324 that names the spell and a SPLPROT row (row in parameter 2, value in parameter 1). In the SR lab's SPLPROT.2DA, row 110 is SPLSTATE=n, so a creature in that spell state is immune: state 41 for Charm Person, Dire Charm and Mental Domination, 29 for Hold Person, 106 for Horror. Rows can combine two others: stat 0x103 means either row, 0x104 neither. SR's Fireball uses row 186, fire resistance from 100 to 127. (CHOICE-01)
- **[src]** The engine evaluates a SPLPROT row in `CRuleTables::IsProtectedFromSpell(row, stats, mine, value)`, non-zero when `stats` meets it. EEex's Lua bindings expose it as `m_ruleTables:IsProtectedFromSpell(...)` on `EEex_EngineGlobal_CBaldurChitin.m_pObjectGame` (InfinityLoader's binding list). The engine's opcode 326 (2.7.3.0) passes the creature receiving the effect as `stats` and the effect's source as `mine` (none when the source is no sprite), and applies its spell when the answer is non-zero. EEex's own opcode 319 modes pass the creature as both. (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** Through EEex's bindings the check answered 0 or 1 as the creatures' values say: EA, general, race, current hit points and spell state; SOURCE held only with the same creature as `stats` and `mine`, and ALLIES and ENEMIES were judged from `mine`'s side. In batches E6 and E7 the adapters read 7,635 such marks in 24 fights, and none failed. With the states set, the engine's Hold Person never went to a Free Action dummy (18 casts) nor its charms to a Chaotic Commands dummy (28 casts). (CHOICE-01)
- **[lab]** Opcode 328 applied with special 0 did not set the SPLSTATE.IDS state (`EEex_Sprite_GetSpellState` read false); with special 1 it did. Spell Revisions sets its states with special 1 (Chaotic Commands: parameter 2 41, special 1). (CHOICE-01)
- **[host]** The classifier reads 57 wizard and priest spells of the SR lab as a weapon or on-hit buff on the caster. Opcode 111 creates a weapon whose damage sits on the created item; the classifier follows it into the item (D2c). Opcodes 248 and 249 add effects to the caster's hits. Among them are the touch attacks (Cause Light Wounds to Cause Mortal Wounds, Harm, Slay Living, Shocking Grasp, Chill Touch, Ghoul Touch, Lich Touch), conjured or thrown weapons (Magical Stone, Melf's Minute Meteors, Spiritual Hammer, Flame Blade, Black Blade of Disaster) and the shapeshifts. The engine casts and strikes with the touch charges (D2c); the rest SCS still casts in rounds the engine leaves to it. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[host]** SR's touch hands are items of type 28 whose one hit casts a sub-spell (opcode 146) and removes the hand (opcode 112): `LIGHT`, `MODERATE`, `SERIOUS`, `CRITICAL`, `HARM`, `SLAYLIVE`, `SGRASP` and `CHILLT`. All but `CHILLT` carry the magical flag (0x40); `CHILLT` (flags 0x1000328) does not, so Protection from Magical Weapons does not stop it. `HARM`'s sub-spell `SPPR608D` deals 90 magic damage and keeps the target at one hit point or more (opcode 208). Melf's Minute Meteors (`MELFMET`) is a ranged weapon of 5 to 20 meteors by level. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[src]** Menu spells: opcode 214 lets the caster pick a sub-spell from a list (a 2DA, or its own spells). In the SR lab four wizard spells do: Protection from Elemental Energy (`SPWI422`: Protection from Fire, Cold, Electricity or Acid), Polymorph Self, Shapechange and Nahal's Reckless Dweomer. The classifier marks them as a choice without roles, so the engine casts none of them, and SCS's AI scripts have no block for them either. Creature files carry the elemental protections as the sub-spells themselves: Protection from Fire is memorized in 125 creature files, Protection from Elemental Energy in one. SCS pre-buffs with copies of the sub-spells (`DWSW319` …) and of the level-7 Protection from the Elements (`DWSW702`), which has no menu. Polymorph Self is memorized in 25 creature files, Nahal's in 17 and Shapechange in 2. Whether opcode 214 does anything for a creature no player controls is untested. (DEFENCE-01)
- **[src]** SCS's troll finishing chains and SR's Greater Command go more than eight spell or EFF deliveries deep (all fit in twelve); some spells cast themselves again through a sub-spell, so a reader needs cycle detection. (SPELLS-01)
- **[src]** Reading resources in game: `EEex_Resource_Fetch(resref, ext)` then `res:Demand()` gives the file's bytes (`EEex_UDToPtr` of the result is the file start, `res.nSize` its size). CRes exposes no Release, so EEex never releases what it demands. InfinityLoader's raw reads (`EEex_ReadU8/U16/U32`, `EEex_Read16/32`, `EEex_ReadLString`, which stops at a NUL) read any address unchecked, so the caller must bound each read by the size. EEex's `EEex_Resource_GetItemAbility` steps by the item header's size, not the 56-byte ability, so it returns wrong abilities after the first. (SPELLS-01)
- **[host]** The in-game reader (`adapters/eeex/resources.lua`), fed the real files through a fake EEex memory, returns exactly the host parser's records for every SPL, ITM, EFF and PRO of both lab games and of Christopher's two installs, with no read outside a resource. (SPELLS-01)
- **[lab]** In the SR lab game the reader matched the host parser on every SPL, EFF and PRO file, 4,930 of 4,932 items and all 435 projectile numbers. The two items differed in dice sides (-1 in the file, 0 in game) only after a save was loaded: the engine's copy of an item can change at load, and the reader sees that copy. Profiles of 11 creatures took at most 29 ms, with no missing record. (SPELLS-01)
- **[src]** Engine bindings for a creature's kit: `sprite.m_equipment.m_items:get(slot)` for slots 0–38 in SLOTS.IDS order (per IESDP 15–17 quick items, 18–33 backpack, 34 magic weapon, 35–38 weapons); a `CItem` has `cResRef`, `m_useCount1..3` (charges, or the stack size, of its first three abilities) and `m_flags`. `sprite:GetQuickButtons(2, false)` and `(4, false)` build the spell and innate menus and must be freed. The items and the selected weapon were read in game (DEFENCE-01); the quick buttons not yet. (SPELLS-01)
